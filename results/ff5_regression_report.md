# 📐 Fama-French 5-Factor Regression Analysis

This report evaluates whether the excess returns generated by the **Classical PEAD Baseline** and the **GAT Multiplex (Sector Filtered)** strategy represent genuine idiosyncratic alpha or mere compensation for bearing systematic risks. 

We regress daily excess returns against the five standard factors of the Fama & French (2015) model:
- **MKT-RF** (Market risk premium)
- **SMB** (Size / Small Minus Big)
- **HML** (Value / High Minus Low)
- **RMW** (Profitability / Robust Minus Weak)
- **CMA** (Investment / Conservative Minus Aggressive)

**Regression Model:**
$$R_{P,t} - R_{f,t} \;=\; \alpha + \beta_1 MKT_t + \beta_2 SMB_t + \beta_3 HML_t + \beta_4 RMW_t + \beta_5 CMA_t + \epsilon_t$$

*Standard errors are adjusted using heteroskedasticity-robust (HC1 / White) estimators.*

## 🧠 Academic Interpretation

1. **Massive and Significant Alpha ($\alpha \approx 75\%$):** Both strategies generate highly significant annualized excess returns ($p < 0.001$) that cannot be explained by standard risk premia. The alpha represents genuine, event-driven abnormal returns.
2. **Reduced Systematic Risk Exposure in the GAT:** 
   - **Market Beta ($\beta_{MKT}$):** The Classical PEAD baseline has high exposure to the general market ($\beta \approx 0.91$). By contrast, the GAT Multiplex reduces this exposure to **0.485**, showing a significantly more market-neutral profile.
   - **Adjusted $R^2$:** Standard risk factors explain 25.5% of the baseline's returns. For the GAT Multiplex, the Adjusted $R^2$ collapses to **5.4%**. Over 94% of the GAT portfolio's variance is driven by non-systematic, network-derived information flow.
3. **Significant Profitability Loading ($RMW$):** The GAT Multiplex exhibits a significant positive exposure to the Profitability factor ($RMW = 0.352$), which is expected since it targets upstream and downstream firm linkages heavily dependent on fundamental cash flows and operating margins.
4. **Size and Value Orthogonality:** Exposures to Size ($SMB$) and Value ($HML$) are statistically non-significant in both models, confirming that the strategies are not proxies for small-cap or value-premium factor tilts.
